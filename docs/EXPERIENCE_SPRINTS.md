# Experience Sprints

> We stop measuring functions. We start measuring experiences.

## The change

A sprint is no longer a list of features. It is **one experience** with a
**binary pass/fail bar**, measured on the founder's real life — not on a demo,
not on a seed.

If the experience fails, the sprint fails, no matter how much code shipped.

## The format

Every sprint is a single question of the form:

> **"Can Andrei ____ in/under ____ ?"**

- **One sentence.** If it needs a paragraph, it's two sprints.
- **A real scenario.** Phone, real project (GOCO / ECUX / RNZ Memory), real
  conditions (on the move, distracted, 30 seconds to spare).
- **A hard threshold.** Time, taps, or a yes/no. No "it feels better."
- **Pass/fail only.** We don't partially pass.

## The first three (Andrei's own)

### Sprint 6 — Launch from a restaurant
> **"Can Andrei launch GOCO while eating at a restaurant in Brașov?"**

**Pass:** on a phone, one sentence + one tap starts a real, correctly-scoped
mission; the brain is visibly working before the food arrives; no keyboard
gymnastics, no desktop, no settings.

### Sprint 7 — Approve in 10 seconds
> **"Can Andrei approve a Claude execution in under 10 seconds?"**

**Pass:** from notification to approved, ≤ 10s. The approval screen shows _what
changed and why_ at a glance — the decision is obvious without reading logs.

### Sprint 8 — Whole portfolio in 30 seconds
> **"Can Andrei understand the state of every company in under 30 seconds?"**

**Pass:** one screen, ≤ 30s, and Andrei can correctly say what's blocked, what's
waiting on him, and what shipped today — across all companies.

## Candidate backlog (unordered)

- "Can Andrei say _'Continue GOCO'_ and get the right work, with zero context
  supplied?" — this is **Milestone Alpha** (see its own doc), the one that
  matters most.
- "Can Andrei go from idea to first deliverable without ever seeing the word
  'worker', 'provider' or 'prompt'?"
- "Can Andrei trust the brain's summary enough to approve without opening
  Advanced Mode?"
- "Can Andrei resume a 3-week-old project and feel like he never left?"

## How we run one

1. Write the one-sentence question + the hard threshold.
2. Walk the scenario _as Andrei_, on a phone, on a real project.
3. Find the first place it breaks. Fix only what unblocks the experience.
4. Re-walk. Pass or fail. No code that doesn't serve the walk.

## What this kills

- Feature work with no scenario behind it.
- "Nice to have" tabs and settings.
- Polish on screens the founder rarely sees.

Build for the walk. Delete everything that isn't on it.
